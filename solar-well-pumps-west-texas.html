How a solar well pump works

Solar panels feed a controller, the controller runs the pump, and the pump lifts water while the sun is up. Because the pump works in daylight, most setups pair it with a storage tank so cattle still have water through cloudy stretches and overnight. No fuel to haul and no power line to run out to the pasture.

  • Daily water need
  • Distance from the well to the trough

Frequently asked questions

What is a solar well pump?

A solar well pump uses solar panels to run a water well pump, so you can water livestock where there is no power line. Cross Buck Construction installs solar panel well setups for ranches and farms across West Texas and Eastern New Mexico, from Wolfforth and Lubbock to Lamesa, Midland and Hobbs.

How much does a solar well pump cost?

It depends on the well depth and water level, how much water your herd needs each day, the pump and panel size, storage tank size, and the distance to the trough. Cross Buck Construction sizes the system to your well first; send those details and call (806) 496-6999 for a free quote.

Can a solar pump replace an old windmill?

Often it can. A solar pump can be sized to the well and to the herd's daily water needs, and paired with a storage tank for cloudy stretches. Cross Buck Construction looks at the well, the existing setup and the water you need, then tells you straight whether solar fits.

What do you need to quote a solar well pump?

Send the town, the well location, the well depth if you know it, how many head of livestock you water, and photos of the well and any existing tank or trough. Text it to (806) 496-6999 and Cross Buck Construction will tell you straight what the job needs.

Can you also run the line and set the trough?

Yes. Cross Buck Construction also does ranch and farm plumbing, water lines and livestock troughs, plus the dirt work to trench a line. One outfit can handle the solar well setup, the line and the trough, so you are not coordinating several contractors on a remote place.
