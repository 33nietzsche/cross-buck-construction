Frequently asked questions

Does Cross Buck Construction wire shops and barns?

Yes. Cross Buck Construction is a licensed electrical shop that also does general contracting and emergency repairs, and electrical comes first. We wire shops, barns and houses across West Texas and Eastern New Mexico, with the electrical work done by Zach, a licensed electrician. That covers service upgrades, new installations, circuits, lighting, outlets, motors, pumps and troubleshooting, and every job is scoped individually with safety first.

Can you run power from the house or meter to a barn or shop?

Yes. Cross Buck Construction runs underground or overhead feeders from the house or meter to barns, shops and outbuildings, sized for the load you plan to run. Because we run our own dirt work equipment, trenching the feeder and wiring the building can be one job.

What if my shop needs more power for a welder or new equipment?

An electrician first checks whether your existing panel and service can carry the added load. If they cannot, the job may need a dedicated circuit, a subpanel or a service upgrade. Cross Buck Construction checks that before quoting and tells you straight what is needed.

Do you troubleshoot electrical problems in an existing shop or barn?

Yes. Cross Buck Construction troubleshoots circuits that trip, dead outlets, lights that fail, and motors or pumps that will not start. Tell us what fails and send photos of the panel area if you can take them safely; do not open energized equipment yourself.

What should I send to get a quote on shop or barn electrical?

Send your town, whether it is a shop, barn, house or mix, what fails or what you want added, and photos of the panel area and the space if you can take them safely. Call or text (806) 496-6999 for a free quote.

Do you do shop and barn electrical outside Lubbock?

Yes. Cross Buck Construction is based in Wolfforth, Texas and does electrical work in Lubbock, Brownfield, Lamesa, Midland, Odessa, Big Spring, Snyder, Sweetwater, Amarillo and Muleshoe, Texas, and Hobbs, New Mexico.
