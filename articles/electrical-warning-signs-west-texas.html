Electrical warning signs in West Texas and Eastern New Mexico homes, shops and barns

Flicker, trip, buzz, smell — when to stop and call

What are the warning signs of an electrical problem at home?

Short answer: The Electrical Safety Foundation International (ESFI) lists these warning signs: lights that flicker or dim, breakers that trip often, outlets or switch plates that feel warm or look discolored, buzzing or sizzling sounds, a burning smell, and mild shocks or tingles when you touch an appliance (ESFI; ESFI checklist PDF). If you notice any of them, stop using that circuit and call a licensed electrician.

What should I do if a breaker keeps tripping?

Unplug what’s on that circuit and reset the breaker once. If it trips again, leave it off and call an electrician. A breaker that keeps tripping is a warning sign on ESFI’s list, not something to keep resetting.

Why do shop and barn circuits give trouble on ranch properties?

Shops and barns often run motors, welders, pumps and heaters, and those loads are heavier than a house’s. A circuit that wasn’t sized for the equipment on it is a reason to have it looked at. Cross Buck Construction wires shop and barn power and motor and pump circuits.

Does outdoor electrical equipment need special ratings?

Yes. Outdoor equipment should be rated for outdoor use. For example, the U.S. Department of Energy says outdoor EV charger installations require outdoor-rated equipment (U.S. DOE Alternative Fuels Data Center).
