When to call an electrician vs. do it yourself (Texas and New Mexico)

Not legal advice. Check your city.

Do I need a licensed electrician for electrical work in Texas?

Short answer: Generally, yes. TDLR says anyone who performs, or offers to perform, non-exempt electrical work in Texas must be licensed and do that work through a licensed electrical contractor (TDLR Electricians).

Can a Texas homeowner do electrical work on their own home?

Texas law has limited exemptions, including one for an owner-occupant working on their own home. Cities can add their own permit and inspection rules on top of that (TDLR – Exemptions). Check with your city before you start.

Can a New Mexico homeowner do their own electrical work?

New Mexico offers a homeowner’s permit for an owner-occupant’s primary residence. The permit has requirements, including passing a homeowner exam (14.5.2.18 NMAC – Homeowner’s Permit). Anyone doing electrical work for someone else needs a CID license (NM RLD – Construction Industries).

What’s safe to handle yourself?

Resetting a tripped breaker once, testing a GFCI outlet with its test/reset buttons, and swapping a light bulb are everyday homeowner tasks. Stop and call a licensed electrician if you see ESFI warning signs: burning smell, warm or discolored outlets, sparks, buzzing, or a breaker that trips again (ESFI).

When should I call an electrician?

Call for panel or service upgrades, new circuits, well pump or motor wiring, shop and barn power, and any of the warning signs above.
