Panel upgrades and EV chargers: straight answers

Load first, then the quote

Do I need a panel upgrade to install a Level 2 EV charger?

Short answer: Maybe. Level 2 chargers run on 240 volts. The U.S. Department of Energy says some homes don’t have enough electrical capacity for Level 2 equipment, and a qualified electrician can tell you whether yours does and add circuits if needed (U.S. DOE Alternative Fuels Data Center).

Can I just use the charger that came with my EV?

Often, yes. DOE notes that many drivers meet their daily needs by charging overnight on a Level 1 cordset, as long as a dedicated branch-circuit outlet is near where they park (U.S. DOE Alternative Fuels Data Center).

What code applies to EV charger installation?

The National Electrical Code treats EV charging as a continuous load. NEC Article 625 covers most charging-equipment requirements, and local permits may be required (U.S. DOE Alternative Fuels Data Center).

When does a home or shop need a service upgrade?

When the planned load, like a new AC unit, shop equipment, a welder or an EV charger, is more than the existing panel or service can carry. An electrician works that out with a load calculation.
